Square A1 · Writing · Kit: hostile-reading

Will this draft survive a hostile referee?

1The problem

A manuscript goes to a journal once. Colleagues read drafts kindly, and often late. The first hostile reading tends to come from the referee, when it costs a rejection.

2The principle

Get the hostile reading before the editor does. The panel prepares. The author decides.

3The workflow

  1. An editor reads for fit with the target journal.
  2. Four referees read blind, each from a different position in the field.
  3. Every comment is classed FATAL, ADDRESSABLE or TASTE.
  4. The author revises and answers each comment in a response table.
  5. Rounds repeat until the draft passes the mark of 80.

4Where it fails

The panel shares the model’s blind spots. It does not know what a referee knows from the archive.

A mark is not a judgment. Passing 80 does not mean a journal will accept the article.

After several rounds a draft can drift toward the panel’s taste and away from the author’s argument.

5A case

The kit’s worked example runs the panel on a published article: Pedahzur and Perliger, “The Changing Nature of Suicide Attacks: A Social Network Perspective,” Social Forces (2006). It shows every comment the panel raised and how each was classed.

6Adapt it

In the kit: three prompts, a review report template, a response table, and the worked example. No installation. Paste into any chat model.

  1. Set your field and target journal.
  2. Set your four referee profiles.
  3. Set your pass mark.
